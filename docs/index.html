04 / HOW I WORKSOFTWARE FIRST · REPRODUCIBLE BY DESIGN

Build small.
Measure clearly.

01

Start with a question

Define a narrow research question, identify assumptions and review relevant literature before interpreting results.

02

Establish a baseline

Use public or synthetic data first; compare against simple, transparent methods before adding complexity.

03

Make it reproducible

Keep data handling, code, parameters and evaluation criteria documented so each step can be inspected.

05 / SELECTED READING NOTESPRIMARY SOURCES · PROJECT CONTEXT

Ideas behind
the methods.

Short notes on papers that inform the current projects. The cited studies provide context and methods; they do not validate the results of my own simulations or EEG analyses.

NEURAL POPULATION MODELS · 1972

Wilson & Cowan

“Excitatory and inhibitory interactions in localized populations of model neurons.” Biophysical Journal, 12(1), 1–24.

The paper formulates coupled equations for local excitatory and inhibitory populations and analyzes the dynamics those interactions can produce. It is the mathematical starting point for the Wilson–Cowan simulation here; the simulation’s chosen parameters are not biological measurements.

Open paper ↗

EEG DEEP LEARNING · 2018

Lawhern et al.

“EEGNet: a compact convolutional neural network for EEG-based brain–computer interfaces.” Journal of Neural Engineering, 15(5), 056013.

Introduces a compact CNN using temporal and depthwise spatial filtering for EEG-based BCI paradigms. It informs the non-graph EEGNet comparator in my study; the 70.1% score shown above is from my own evaluation, not the paper.

Open paper ↗

BCI SYSTEMS & EVALUATION · 2002

Wolpaw et al.

“Brain–computer interfaces for communication and control.” Clinical Neurophysiology, 113(6), 767–791.

Reviews signal selection, translation into device commands, user training and objective performance evaluation. It helps frame the signal-to-control stages in my closed-loop simulation; that synthetic software model is not a tested BCI.
