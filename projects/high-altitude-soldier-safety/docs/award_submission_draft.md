## Project title

Offline Signal-Quality-Aware Wearable Logger and Separate Backpack Load Measurement Demonstrator

## Problem being investigated

High-altitude and cold environments create documented risks for personnel. This project investigates a narrow engineering question: can a low-cost offline prototype prevent low-quality sensor input from being presented as a reliable trend, and can a separate bench module measure backpack load against a user-set study limit?

This is a research question, not proof that soldiers currently lack monitoring or that this prototype reduces illness or injury.

## Proposed solution

A local software demonstrator applies a transparent signal-quality gate, stores selected values offline, and displays generic re-check or wearer check-in prompts. The separate load-cell module is planned; current code contains only an isolated calibration conversion helper and no physical load cell has been built or tested. No disease labels, treatment recommendations, or duty-fitness decisions are produced.

## Impact

Potential impact is a hypothesis. No health outcome, injury prevention, cost saving, soldier acceptance, or operational benefit has been measured.

## Risks and limits

- Not a medical device; not validated to diagnose or predict disease.
- A sensor at one body site cannot monitor an entire body.
- Surface temperature cannot identify frostbite or organ damage.
- A load display does not reduce load or establish ergonomic benefit.
- The concept has not been evaluated with soldiers or in high-altitude conditions.
